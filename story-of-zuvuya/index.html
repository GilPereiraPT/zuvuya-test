How Zuvuya Came to Be

I bought my first two astrology books at the Theosophical Bookstore on Capitol Hill in Seattle, Washington: Esoteric Astrology, scribed by Alice Bailey, and Encyclopedia of Medical Astrology by H. L. Cornell, MD.

Anyone can read Esoteric Astrology. There are no glyphs, no symbols, or astrological equations. It presents Astrology in such a way as to put most traditional views of astrology on their heads. It was a good place for me to start.

This was not the case with the Encyclopedia. It is a mixture of words, symbols and astrological correlations. I simply couldn't read it, so I went looking for an Astrology 101 class.

Astrology Et Al had a storefront in the University District. Jeff Green's class caught my eye. At the time, my first husband and I had been part of a small Gurdjieff group, and a class on Evolutionary Astrology was much more intriguing than the 101 series.

I asked if I could sign up and was discouraged from doing so without the basic series. Duly noting the day and time, I showed up and asked Jeff if I could sit in. He said fine. I took a seat.

Jeff was seven years away from catching the wave to fame. His classes were lightly attended. I asked a lot of questions that usually began with “What do you mean by…”. Shortly after, we became a couple.

I attended numerous classes and astrology conferences. I met some very fine and diverse astrologers. In 1985, Pluto: The Evolutionary Journey of the Soul was published and was outstandingly well received.

In 1988 we separated and I moved to Northern California. In 1993 I was gifted a copy of the Dreamspell.

What I hadn't been able to find through Astrology I found through the Dreamspell.

“Astrology maps the sky around you.
